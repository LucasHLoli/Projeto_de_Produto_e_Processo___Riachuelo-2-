\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\noindent In partnership with Riachuelo, the team developed an anti-theft tag that customers release themselves after paying with their phone, with the goal of reducing checkout queues in fashion retail. The market definition set the target segment as mass-market fashion chains that already use self-checkout. Consumer research, with a 142-response survey and eleven interviews, identified four customer groups, and only 24\% of respondents endorsed a purchase without a manual check at the exit. The findings were deployed into seven technical requirements through a house of quality, in which payment-conditioned release, payment time and the clarity of the flow accounted for about 71\% of the technical importance. In the conceptual design, functional modelling defined the overall function, reconciling garment and payment, and broke it down into 18 secondary and auxiliary functions. The differentiation study compared six existing solutions, the vertical price scale indicated a market value between R\$~5 and R\$~8 per tag, and the morphological matrix produced three design alternatives. Commercialization was outlined as direct sales to the retail chain, with the tags supplied under lease and a pilot in one store. In the detailed design, the concept was revised: the garment is held by a pin and a ball clutch, a micro-actuator moves magnets under the clutch once the server confirms the payment, a 2D code engraved on the lid identifies the device, three LEDs show its state and the cell is recharged by induction. The assembly measures 120~$\times$~60~$\times$~30~mm and does not meet the specified envelope in length and height. The product structure has 20 components and 27 parts. A DFMA analysis proposed eight changes that reduce the device to 16 parts, cut the estimated assembly time from 199 to 106~s and raise the DFA index from 0.21 to 0.37. The materials favour recyclable polymers and aluminium, and the macro process plan organizes assembly into six stations.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fashion retail; anti-theft tag; self-checkout; product development; DFMA.
