\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\noindent In partnership with Riachuelo, the team developed the concept of an anti-theft tag that customers release themselves after paying with their phone, with the goal of reducing checkout queues in fashion retail. The market definition set the target segment as mass-market fashion chains that already use self-checkout. Consumer research, with a 142-response survey and eleven interviews, identified four customer groups, and only 24\% of respondents endorsed a purchase without a manual check at the exit. The findings were deployed into seven technical requirements through a house of quality, in which payment-conditioned release, payment time and the clarity of the flow accounted for about 71\% of the technical importance. In the conceptual design, functional modelling defined the overall function, reconciling garment and payment, and broke it down into 18 functions. The differentiation study compared six existing solutions and identified, as the differentiating factor, purchase by phone without giving up either the physical retention of the garment or human assistance. The vertical price scale, applied to three retail professionals, indicated a market value between R\$~5 and R\$~8 per tag, a result that is still indicative. The technical benchmarking study gathered 126 effect carriers in an idea gallery, grouped into six lines of similarity. The morphological matrix produced three design alternatives, and the chosen one keeps the pin with a ball clutch and adopts release by a spring with a trigger, authorization by tapping the phone, indication by an LED ring and inductive charging. Commercialization was outlined as direct sales to the retail chain, with the tags supplied under lease and a pilot in one store. The redesigned device has not yet been dimensioned, which is left to the detailed design stage.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fashion retail; anti-theft tag; self-checkout; conceptual design; morphological matrix.
